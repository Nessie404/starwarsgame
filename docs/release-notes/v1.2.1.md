**Download `wiikart.zip`**, unzip it, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) — or copy the `apps` folder to an SD
card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel on a real Wii.

A stability release. Same game as v1.2.0, with the bugs a full audit of
the code turned up. Every fix below is pinned by a test that fails
without it.

### Falling off the edge could win you the race

Rebuilding a respawning car's progress as `lap × segments + checkpoint`
looks right, but the lap counter ticks over while the car is in the air.
Go over the edge in the last two segments before the line and you were
returned to a checkpoint a lap behind — and credited with **96% of a
lap**. On a two-lap circuit that won the race outright from the middle of
lap one. A respawn now moves the car back by the arc it actually gave up,
and the lap is derived from that. The test sweeps twelve segments either
side of the line on both unguarded circuits.

### Crossing the line stopped your car and reversed it

A finished human kart fell through to the AI driver, which then ran it on
AI fields a human kart never had: zero skill and zero corner confidence
read as "brake for everything". Measured, your car stood on the brakes
after the flag and drove **backwards at 21 km/h** down the circuit,
through the cars still racing. A finished car now coasts.

### Menus dropped most keyboard presses

The menu keys were read out of the held-key map on every tenth frame, so
a normal tap that began and ended between two of those frames did nothing
at all — the menus looked frozen. W/A/S/D are now edges, like Enter and
Esc always were, with a slow auto-repeat if you hold one down.

### The controller check now knows whether a keyboard exists

`KEYBOARD_Init` succeeds whether or not a keyboard is plugged in — it
starts the driver, and the device search happens later — so the v1.2.0
count treated players 1 and 2 as always seated. A 2-player race could
start with one Wii Remote, leaving player 2 a statue and the race with no
end, and the mid-race "controller lost" watchdog could never fire for the
1- and 2-player cases it was written for. Presence now comes from the
keyboard's own connect/disconnect events.

### And the rest

- The drop off a cliff is visible again: the fall wrote the car's height
  and the road-surface code overwrote it on the same frame, so the car
  slid along at road level and then teleported. It now falls about 11 m
  before Lakitu-duty picks it up.
- A negative checkpoint index could read off the front of the checkpoint
  array — `%` keeps the sign of its left operand, so it only guarded the
  top end. Clamped both ways.
- NaN dies at the door: `game_clampf` returns the low bound for one
  (`v < lo` is false for NaN, so it used to sail straight through into a
  position or a heading and stick there), `game_angle_wrap` bounds its
  input (past about 1e9 a float's step exceeds 2π and its loop could
  never terminate), and a non-finite frame time is refused.
- Rumble is silenced on every channel when a race is abandoned, so
  quitting mid-buzz no longer leaves a pad running for the session.
- The dropped-controller grace timer resets between races.
- The results screen classifies all twelve cars instead of the first
  eight.
- A track can no longer declare more power-up rows than the array holds.

### Clean

The audit also found no out-of-bounds indexing in the track builder (all
five circuits sit comfortably inside every declared maximum), no NaN or
division by zero reachable from any finite input, no unbounded loop in
the hot path, no uninitialised state surviving a restart, and no menu row
index that can be pushed out of range — including by reducing the player
count while the cursor is on a garage row.
