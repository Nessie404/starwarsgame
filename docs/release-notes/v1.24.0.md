# WiiKart 1.24.0

Banked corners, a bigger BULLRING, cars that hold a line, a rebuilt
car designer, and a couple of long-overdue readability fixes.

## Road banking, everywhere

Every circuit's curves now cant into the turn. On the seven mountain
passes and CLASSIC it is subtle — a small, realistic road crown that
grows with how tight the bend is, the kind of thing a real road
actually has. On BULLRING it is a real, if gentle, bank, deliberately
much stronger than anywhere else in the game, because that circuit is
built to be raced like an oval.

It isn't just for show. A banked corner genuinely lends the tires extra
grip — part of gravity now points toward the corner instead of
straight down, the same reason a real banked turn lets you carry more
speed through it. BULLRING's turns are where this actually matters.

## BULLRING, bigger

Both straights are now 400 m each, twice what they were — a short
track grown into a proper speedway, with real banking in the turns to
match the extra room to build speed on the way in.

## Cars hold a line much longer before losing it

The understeer limit used to be a hard wall: ask for more rotation
than the tires could deliver and the car simply stopped turning that
much, scrubbing speed immediately. There is now a shoulder past that
limit — pushed a little harder, a car genuinely turns tighter instead
of just being capped, before it actually lets go.

Losing it outright — a real spin, not just running wide — now has two
distinct causes instead of one:

- **Big torque, on lock, in a tight turn.** A rear-driven car's rear
  axle can still step out for free under hard cornering power, same as
  before. All-wheel-drive cars can now be provoked into it too, but
  need a genuinely harder commitment first — the front axle sharing
  the load keeps an AWD car planted well past where a rear-driven one
  would already be loose. Front-driven cars still never spin from
  power alone; they push wide instead, the way a real FWD car does.
- **Carrying too much speed into a corner.** Independent of the driven
  axle or the throttle — coming in hot enough that no amount of
  discipline saves it, for any drivetrain.

## The car designer, rebuilt

Mass is no longer a dial of its own. It now follows power directly —
more engine always costs more weight — so a design can't have a
superkart's power-to-weight and a truck's horsepower at the same time;
pushing power up is a real trade-off, not a free upgrade.

In exchange, gearing is now fully in your hands: gear count is
adjustable, and each gear's own top speed and upshift/downshift point
can be dialed in individually, instead of one auto-derived ladder.

## Cars look like their numbers, and yours is easy to find

A car's model — on track and on the garage turntable — now varies with
its actual spec: a long wheelbase reads as a long car, more mass as a
broader one, more drag as a taller, boxier one, and the driven axle
carries visibly bigger tires. Eleven cars on a circuit no longer look
like one shape in different paint.

In single-player, your own car's dot on the minimap now carries a
pulsing white ring, so picking it out of the field is instant instead
of a squint at colours mid-corner.

## Playing it

Download `wiikart.zip`, unzip, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) (File > Open). On a real Wii, copy the
`apps` folder to an SD card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel.

To edit `cars.json`, `settings.json` or `controls.json` in Dolphin you need
an SD card: Config > Wii > Insert SD Card, put the `apps` folder on it, and
launch `boot.dol` from there. The JSON CONFIG screen on the main menu tells
you what was actually read.
