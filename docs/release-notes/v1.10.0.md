### The rivals are people now

Skill and personality used to be the same thing: the strategy sheet a car
drove to decided both *how* it raced and *how quick* it ultimately was, so
every cautious driver was slow and every aggressive one was fast, and the
field was eleven variations of quite good.

Each of the eleven now has their own:

Name, colour and trait are fixed to the grid slot, so the same rival turns
up every race — and the trait now appears beside their name on the results
screen.

### What each number actually does

- **Skill** is how close to the limit they dare run. Measured on Berthoud
  with everything else held equal: 65.0 s a lap at 0.86 skill, 60.7 s at
  1.06. The strategy sheets no longer carry a pace ceiling or an engine
  trim, so this is where speed comes from.
- **Nerve** (consistency) is how often they get it wrong. A ragged driver
  carries a few percent more into a corner than the tires will hold, runs
  wide, and loses confidence for it.
- **Aggression** decides how often they gamble on an unguarded corner.
  Measured on Monarch: the three wildest drivers gambled eight times
  between them, the seven steady ones four.
- **Tire care** picks their compound and scales their wear — the gentle
  drivers run softs, the ones who abuse rubber run hards.

### Breadcrumbs, if you want to take this further

`docs/HANDOFF.md` is new: how to build and test without a Wii toolchain,
what every file does, the conventions that keep the project honest, and a
start-here recipe for each remaining TODO item.

It includes an honest note about what is *not* finished here. A driver's
lap time is still dominated by their sheet's constant line offset — the
INSIDE line is shorter, so it is quicker, whoever drives it. Fixing that
means building a real apex-based racing line, and the handoff explains
exactly where and how, along with the test that would prove it worked.
