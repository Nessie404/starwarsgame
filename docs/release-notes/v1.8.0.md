### Rubber that has a life

Each compound has a temperature it wants to run at and a window either
side of it, a rate it heats up at, a rate it cools down at, a wear rate,
and how much grip that wear costs. Tires heat with work — rolling, and
especially sliding — and cool with speed, so a long straight brings a hot
set back down. Cold rubber, overheated rubber and worn rubber are all
short of grip.

A parked car does no work, so it neither heats nor wears its tires. That
sounds obvious; the first version of the model had a stationary car
cooking its tires to 260°C.

### Softs are no longer the answer

The whole field on one compound, whole races, measured:

| Circuit | Soft | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic — 4 laps, a sprint | **127.3 s** | 128.7 s | 130.7 s |
| Kenosha — 2 laps, the long one | 267.6 s | **260.0 s** | 263.4 s |
| Guanella — 2 laps of switchbacks | 190.7 s | 187.7 s | **187.2 s** |

Softs win the sprint and wear out completely in the long race. Hards give
up grip but roll better and barely wear. Each compound wins somewhere,
and a regression test fails if softs ever quietly become the automatic
choice again.

The AI feel it too — cold, worn or overheated tires slow their corner
speeds — so a compound choice shows up in the race rather than only on
your own car.

### On the HUD

Compound, temperature, and a life bar. The temperature reads amber and
then red as the rubber leaves its window; the bar empties as it wears and
turns amber, then red, as it runs out.

### Fresh rubber means fresh

The fresh-rubber power-up now fits an actual new set: wear back to zero,
tires arriving at the temperature they want.

### Also fixed

A car that went off just after the line, got recovered just before it, and
drove over it again was credited with the same lap twice — a 1.8-second
lap. A lap now only counts once.

Every number above is in the `tires` block of `settings.json`.
