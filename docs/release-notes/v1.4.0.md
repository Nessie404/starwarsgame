**Download `wiikart.zip`**, unzip it, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) — or copy the `apps` folder to an SD
card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel on a real Wii.

Two things: the camera finally behaves, and the picture is sharper.

### The camera

The whole high-priority camera block from `TODO.md` is done, and it now
lives in its own module with host tests, so its behaviour is measured
rather than eyeballed.

- **Reversing swings the view round.** Past a dead zone, the camera walks
  round toward the nose of the car until it is looking back the way you
  are actually going. It is rate-limited and smoothed, so rocking around a
  standstill can neither snap the view 180 degrees nor set it hunting —
  measured at 0.005 rad per frame against a 2.5 rad/s ceiling, and 0.05 rad
  of total swing while rocking on the dead zone for ten seconds.
- **Driving forward brings it back** the same smooth way.
- **It follows the road.** The camera copies a configurable share of the
  road's pitch, so a climb tilts the view up the hill and a descent tilts
  it down: measured +0.6° on a 17% climb and +13.8° on a 19% descent,
  against +6.8° on the flat. Smoothed, so a bump is not a nod.
- **It stays out of trouble.** Pitch is clamped at both ends, the camera
  keeps its ground clearance over any terrain (1.6 m, verified over 90
  seconds of driving on all five circuits), and the aim point cannot sink
  into the pavement.
- **Look-ahead grows with speed** — 7.5 m at walking pace, 18 m at 40 m/s
  — and stops at a configured cap.
- **A checkpoint respawn re-places it** instead of letting it streak
  across the mountain after the teleported car.

All of it is tunable in the new `camera` block of `config/settings.json`:
distance, height, clearance, look-ahead and its speed gain and cap, aim
height and floor, follow and aim smoothing, reverse dead zone, full-swing
speed, orbit rate and smoothing, pitch influence, pitch smoothing, and
pitch limits. `config/README.md` documents every key, and a file with
impossible values is rejected whole, leaving the built-in camera running.

### A sharper picture

The Wii's framebuffer is 640 pixels wide and no software can change that.
What this release does is stop throwing half of it away and stop
stretching what is left:

- **480p progressive** is selected when a component cable is present and
  progressive scan is on, instead of always taking the interlaced mode.
  Every line is drawn every frame, so the guardrails stop shimmering and
  the deflicker blur is no longer needed to hide it. In Dolphin, turn on
  **Config → Wii → Progressive Scan (480p)** for the same mode.
- **True 16:9** on a widescreen console: the game renders a 16:9 field of
  view rather than letting a 4:3 image be pulled sideways by the TV, in
  split-screen as well. No "widescreen hack" required in Dolphin.
- **In Dolphin, go further.** Internal Resolution under Graphics renders
  the scene at 1080p, 1440p or 4K, and Enhancements adds anti-aliasing and
  anisotropic filtering. That is where the real visual win is on a PC, and
  it costs the Wii nothing.
