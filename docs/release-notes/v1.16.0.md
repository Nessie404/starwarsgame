# WiiKart 1.16.0

Boost and the fresh-rubber power-up are both gone. No button, no panel,
no charge to manage — a car's power is just its horsepower, and a tire
compound is a bet on the whole race rather than something you top up.

## What's different

There is no `aspiration` block in `cars.json` any more, no boost gauge on
the HUD, and no roadside item panel to drive through. The X/M and B/N
keys, GameCube Y and D-pad up, and their Xbox equivalents are free again.
Tires still heat up and wear out exactly as they did before — that part
of the model is untouched — but nothing ever resets that wear mid-race,
so picking a compound is a decision for the whole distance, not a
resource to spend and collect lap to lap.

`TURBO` and `BLOWER` are gone from the garage: both existed only to
demonstrate the aspiration mechanic, and without it neither car had a
reason of its own to keep — stripped of their multiplier, `TURBO` was a
near-duplicate of `SPORT` and `BLOWER` was a strictly worse `TOURER`.
`RUBY` keeps its own identity (six gears, 1080 kg, high horsepower for
its weight) as a plain naturally-aspirated car.

Removing the tire refresh meant retuning how fast compounds wear: the
old rates assumed the (now-gone) panel would periodically reset a car's
tires, so without it softs wore down to nothing before even a short
sprint finished. Wear is slower across the board now, tuned so a soft
still wins a short race and a medium or hard still wins a long one —
same trade-off as before, just without a pit-stop-in-a-box to lean on.

## Playing it

Download `wiikart.zip`, unzip, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) (File > Open). On a real Wii, copy the
`apps` folder to an SD card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel.

To edit `cars.json`, `settings.json` or `controls.json` in Dolphin you need
an SD card: Config > Wii > Insert SD Card, put the `apps` folder on it, and
launch `boot.dol` from there. The JSON CONFIG screen on the main menu tells
you what was actually read.
