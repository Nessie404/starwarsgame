# WiiKart 1.28.0

Drifting and grip driving, rebuilt from the tire up. Real slip-angle
forces per axle instead of a flat yaw cap, weight transfer that makes
lift-off oversteer free, a handbrake that actually locks the rear
tires instead of just kicking the yaw rate, and a loose-surface bonus
that's the real reason drifting is only quicker off pavement.

## A real dynamic bicycle model

Every corner in WiiKart used to be governed by one number: how much
yaw rate the tires' lateral grip allowed, given the car's speed. Ask
for more with the wheel than that cap allowed and the car understeered
progressively — physically reasonable, but not how a real tire
actually behaves. A real tire's cornering force depends on its *slip
angle*, the gap between the direction it's pointed and the direction
it's actually travelling, and that force rises to a peak somewhere
around 6-10 degrees of slip before falling off — not instantly, but
gradually, settling toward a lower, sliding level that never quite
reaches zero.

That's what the tires do now. Each axle tracks its own slip angle,
computed from the car's real lateral velocity and yaw rate — both of
which are now genuine simulated quantities (`Kart.vy`, `Kart.yaw_rate`)
that the car's position integrates directly, instead of a car's motion
always pointing exactly where its nose does. Push a slip angle past
its peak and the axle's force follows a real curve down toward a
sliding floor, so a sustained slide finds an actual equilibrium speed
and yaw rate instead of either snapping straight back or spinning away
with nothing to stop it.

## Combined slip, split by axle

Braking or accelerating hard while turning already cost cornering grip
(the friction circle, v1.27.0) — now that trade is split the way a
real car's actually is. A driven axle spends part of its own lateral
budget on putting power down, weighted by drivetrain: a front-driven
car understeers more under power than a rear-driven one, an all-wheel
drive car splits the demand and gives some back to both axles. But
braking doesn't care about drivetrain — brakes act on all four wheels
regardless of which axle the engine drives — so a brake application
spends the same lateral budget on both axles together, front and rear,
exactly like a real car's binders.

## Weight transfer, and a handbrake that means something different now

Accelerating loads the rear axle; braking or lifting off the throttle
loads the front. That's not new information, but it's now a real
mechanic: trail-braking or simply lifting mid-corner genuinely shifts
grip toward the front axle and away from the rear, which is exactly
the free, no-handbrake way a real driver provokes a little oversteer
to help the nose turn in.

The handbrake's own job changed to make room for that. It no longer
just kicks the yaw rate and a visual crab angle — it locks the rear
axle directly to its sliding-friction floor, regardless of what the
rear's actual slip angle reads, and it takes the ordinary stability
assist out of the loop while it's held. That's now the one deliberate
way to put the rear tires past their own grip peak on purpose, which
is what starting a real drift means.

Ordinary grip driving still needs a backstop: an AI driver never
touches the handbrake, so an especially violent single-frame input (a
very tight corner taken too fast) could in principle launch the yaw
rate and lateral velocity into a state the slide-force curve alone
wouldn't recover from within a race. A fast stability governor blends
both back toward the grip-limited kinematic reference whenever the
handbrake isn't overriding it — the same real-ESC idea of braking
individual wheels to cut excess yaw directly, standing in for genuine
per-wheel braking the game doesn't model.

## Why drifting is only quicker off pavement

A slid tire past its peak, on a loose surface, gets something a dry
tire on pavement never does: a real, physical bonus from the wedge of
displaced gravel, dirt, or snow it plows up as it slides. WiiKart now
models that directly — off-road, or in snow and ice, a tire past its
slip peak gets extra lateral force from that displaced-material wedge
on top of its ordinary sliding floor. On tarmac there's no material to
displace, so there's no bonus: a hard slide there is pure lost grip,
same as it's always been. That's the actual physical reason drifting
is only genuinely faster than gripping through a corner when the
surface underneath is loose — and now it's true in WiiKart for the
same reason it's true on a real rally stage.

## What this changes for a driver, human or AI

Nothing about the controls changed — steering, throttle, brake, and
the handbrake button are exactly where they were. What changed is what
happens underneath: pushing a corner past the limit now feels like a
real, recoverable slide with its own momentum rather than an
instantaneous understeer scrub, lifting off mid-corner does something
real, and the handbrake commits the rear tires to sliding rather than
just nudging the heading. Every AI driver on the grid runs through the
identical physics — there is no separate, simpler model standing in
for them.
