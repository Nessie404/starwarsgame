### Hills that behave like hills

The road's slope is a tangent, so the gravity pulling a car back down it
is `g*sin(theta)`, not `g*tan(theta)` — worth 4% on Breakneck's steepest
section, and free to get right. The same angle takes weight off the tires
(`m*g*cos(theta)`), so a steep climb now costs a little grip as well as
speed.

Measured, from 43 km/h with the throttle flat for three seconds:

| Road | After 3 s |
|---|---|
| 20% descent | 104.3 km/h |
| flat | 88.8 km/h |
| 20% climb | 73.7 km/h |

Both effects are scalable from a new `hills` block in `settings.json`:
turn `gravity_multiplier` up to make every pass feel more mountainous than
it is, or `load_effect` down if you would rather steep ground did not cost
grip at all.

### Cars that decide their own shift points

`cars.json` gained optional keys:

```json
"automatic_upshift_fraction": 0.95,
"automatic_downshift_fraction": 0.38,
"automatic_upshift_per_gear": [0.70, 0.85, 0.95, 0.99]
```

Fractions are of that gear's own limiter speed, so a car can short-shift
out of first and hold second all the way to the limiter. Shift points
closer than 0.20 apart are refused — that is a gearbox that changes up
into its own downshift point and hunts — and a refused file leaves the
roster exactly as it was.

### One bug worth naming

Adding those fields left the built-in cars with no shift points at all,
which the gearbox read as "change up immediately": a car took top gear at
walking pace and bogged there at 5 km/h. Every car now gets the standard
points unless it says otherwise, applied when a race starts as well as
when a roster is loaded. It was caught by the new test, before the build.
