# WiiKart 1.23.0

A new oval, two cars built for it, smoother corners almost everywhere,
and an in-game car designer.

## BULLRING

The eighth mountain pass would have been one too many, so this one
isn't a pass at all: two long straights joined by two wide, constant-
radius sweeping turns, flat the whole way round, with grandstands
running the length of both straights. It's generated from the oval's
own geometry rather than drawn by hand, specifically so there's
nothing abrupt anywhere on the lap — the smoothest circuit in the game
by a wide margin.

Two new cars are built for it: **STOCKER** and its lower-drag sibling
**SLIPSTREAM**, both huge power and low drag for the straights, with
just enough grip to actually hold BULLRING's turns without banking to
lean on the way a real oval car would. They're genuinely quick there —
noticeably ahead of the roster's other big-power cars — while still
being drivable (if not a great choice) on the mountain passes, low
dirt grip and all.

## Smoother corners, almost everywhere

"Smooth out the curves" turned out to mean two different things. The
track-position relaxation pass that eases every circuit's corners now
blends over a wider neighborhood, which measurably softens how sharply
curvature ramps up and back down entering and exiting a bend — the
hairpins on Berthoud 2.0 and Guanella are exactly as tight as they
were, they just don't arrive and leave as abruptly.

MONARCH is the one exception, deliberately. It's the narrowest,
steepest, most unguarded circuit in the roster, and the wider blend
made a real difference for the worse there: an AI driver on the YOLO
strategy sheet — committed, rarely pays for it — stopped ever
recovering from a fall on the tightest hairpin. MONARCH keeps the
original pass rather than ship a circuit where one in eleven cars can
get permanently stuck.

## The car designer

From the main menu, DESIGN A CAR opens a blank sheet: pick a name from
a list, then dial in mass, power, brakes, grip, drag and dirt grip,
and choose front, rear or all-wheel drive. A live preview shows the
same spec sheet the garage already shows for a stock car — horsepower,
0-100 time, top speed, braking distance, gearing — so you can see what
you're building as you go. Wheelbase and the gear ladder are set for
you from power, drag and mass, the same way STOCKER's and SLIPSTREAM's
were hand-tuned.

SAVE adds the car to the garage for the rest of the session, and — if
`cars.json` was actually found and is writable — saves the whole
roster back to it, so the car is still there next time too.

## Playing it

Download `wiikart.zip`, unzip, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) (File > Open). On a real Wii, copy the
`apps` folder to an SD card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel.

To edit `cars.json`, `settings.json` or `controls.json` in Dolphin you need
an SD card: Config > Wii > Insert SD Card, put the `apps` folder on it, and
launch `boot.dol` from there. The JSON CONFIG screen on the main menu tells
you what was actually read — and, for the car designer, where a saved car
actually went.
