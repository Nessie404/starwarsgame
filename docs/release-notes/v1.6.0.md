### Roads that were designed, not extruded

A circuit can now carry a width profile — a multiplier per control point,
eased between them the same way the road itself is. The point is that a
hairpin can stay **narrow and punishing** while another corner of exactly
the same radius is **opened out** enough to hold a second line and reward
carrying speed through it. That is a road-design decision, not something
curvature should decide on its own.

Berthoud, Loveland, Monarch and both new passes have profiles: the three
tightest corners on each are pinched in, two more of the tight ones are
opened out. Berthoud now runs from 7.2 m to 12.7 m wide, Loveland from
9.0 m to 15.8 m, Monarch from 6.5 m to 11.4 m.

The width is real everywhere it should be: grip and the off-road penalty,
the guardrails, the cliff edge you can fall off, checkpoints, where the
power-up panels sit, the racing line the AI picks — and the renderer, so
what you see is what the physics is using. Park a car 5 m off the
centreline and it does 59.5 km/h on the wide part of Berthoud and 55.8 on
the narrow part, which is the test that proves it.

### Breakneck Pass

Short and mean: **945 m, 44 m of climb, grades to 29%, 7.4 m of road and
no guardrails.** The elevation arrives in steps rather than one smooth
arc — a ramp to a shelf, a steeper ramp to a second shelf, the summit,
then a plunge with a ledge partway down. Three laps.

### Guanella Pass

**1986 m of switchbacks**: 15 corners, seven of them hairpins, the
tightest at a 6 m radius, climbing 64 m on 7.8 m of unguarded road. Modest
overall elevation, relentless direction changes. Two laps.

Three of its hairpins are pinched narrower and two are opened out, so the
switchbacks are not interchangeable — some you thread, some you can attack.

All eleven AI drivers finish both circuits. Best AI laps as measured:
38.6 s round Breakneck, 89.9 s round Guanella.
