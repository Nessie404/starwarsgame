# WiiKart 1.19.1

Berthoud Pass 2.0's real switchbacks are back.

## What happened

v1.17.0 rebuilt Berthoud Pass 2.0's control points, replacing its
original hairpin switchback stack — a climb, a summit, a descent, a
valley loop-back, and a climb home — with a smooth, mathematically
tidy sine-wiggle shape. The reason at the time was a geometric check
that found several pieces of the original road passing within 1-2 m of
each other in a top-down (plan) view. The rebuild fixed that check. It
also quietly took most of the fun out of the circuit: 33 corners down
to 7 m radius became 20 corners with nothing tighter than 37 m.

It turns out that check was never actually catching a gameplay bug.
`track_locate`, the function that figures out where a car is on the
track, is always called with a windowed hint during normal driving and
at the start grid — it only looks at the ±8 segments around where the
car already was, never the whole track. Two switchback tiers landing
close together in plan view but at very different elevations — which
is exactly what a real mountain switchback stack looks like from
above — could never actually get confused with each other at the
wheel. The only place a global (unhinted) search happens is decorative
tree placement, where getting it occasionally wrong is invisible.

## What changed

Berthoud Pass 2.0's original v1.15.0 hairpin layout is back, and
scaled up further (1.30× → 1.50×) for more room across the whole
circuit without smoothing a single turn out of it:

| | v1.15.0 (original) | v1.17.0 (rebuild) | v1.19.1 (this release) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 3263 m | 1411 m | 3704 m |
| Climb | 86 m | 67 m | 85 m |
| Corners | 33 | 20 | 36 |
| Tightest radius | 7 m | 37 m | 8 m |

A new test, `test_berthoud2_keeps_its_switchbacks`, checks corner
count, tightest radius, and total climb directly, so a future geometry
pass can't flatten this out a third time without the test suite
noticing.

## Playing it

Download `wiikart.zip`, unzip, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) (File > Open). On a real Wii, copy the
`apps` folder to an SD card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel.
