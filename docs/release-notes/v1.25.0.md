# WiiKart 1.25.0

Grip that holds until you deliberately let it go, real RPM-based gears,
turbo/supercharger/naturally-aspirated engines, a garage where any car
can be edited, a slowing-down lap that doesn't end in a dead stop, and
every on-screen number now in mph, feet and pounds.

## Grip until it lets go

Holding the wheel over used to trip an escalating power-oversteer
mechanic: cornering grip climbed by up to 40% over about a second, and
if it wasn't "caught" in time the car spun uncontrollably. In practice
that meant turning in and holding a line could suddenly, unexpectedly,
turn much harder than commanded and snap off the road.

That mechanic is gone. Understeer scrub and the tire "shoulder"
(v1.24.0) still apply exactly as before — push past the grip limit and
the car scrubs speed, progressively worse the further past it you are —
but there is no more automatic escalation and no more forced spin from
steering alone. The only way to deliberately break the rear loose is
the handbrake. That's drifting, and it's still there; it just isn't
something that can happen by accident any more.

Two smaller physics fixes ride along with this: the cornering grip
cap's low-speed floor moved from 0.5 to 6 m/s (below a walking pace,
steering geometry is the real limit, not grip — dividing by a near-zero
speed used to let the cap run away), and the guardrail collision
penalty softened from 2.5/s to 1.2/s, so a car that does scrub down
hard against a barrier recovers instead of getting stuck spiraling.

## Real gears

Every car now has one engine curve — a single nominal RPM, shared by
every gear, exactly like a real engine has one torque curve mapped
through different ratios. Power peaks there and falls away steeply,
not gently, to both idle and redline, with a hard cutoff at the
limiter. Automatic shifts follow straight from where that nominal RPM
sits relative to redline. There's no separate shift-point dial any
more — nominal RPM is the one number that matters, and it's the one
thing in the gearbox you can still tune.

## Turbo, supercharged, or neither

Cars can now be naturally aspirated (the default), turbocharged, or
supercharged. A naturally aspirated engine makes no forced-induction
bonus at all — what you see on the power stat is what you get, the
instant the revs are there. A turbo keeps the spool mechanic WiiKart
already had: it builds and bleeds off over real time, genuine lag in
both directions, for the biggest bonus of the three. A supercharger is
driven directly off the engine rather than exhaust flow, so its boost
is instant and tracks revs exactly, with no lag at all — in exchange
for a real bonus, just a smaller one than a turbo's.

## The car designer, again

Grip is no longer a free dial. `designer_grip()` derives it from the
mass the power trade-off already settled on — lighter is grippier — and
from drag, on the theory that a car built with more aero surface is
running some of that as downforce, not just drag. It's the same
"trade-off, not an independent slider" treatment mass itself got in
v1.24.0. The GRIP row still shows the number; you just can't push it
around on its own any more.

## Edit any car, not just new ones

The garage has a new EDIT CAR row. Pick any car — a built-in, or one
you designed earlier — and it opens in the same designer screen used to
build a car from scratch, every stat editable exactly the same way.
The only difference is what SAVE does: instead of adding a new car, it
patches that car's numbers for the rest of the session (the button
reads APPLY, SESSION ONLY, to say so), and nothing is written to disk.
Close the game and it's back to normal next time.

## The slowing-down lap keeps moving

A finished car used to brake all the way to a dead stop and sit there.
Now it eases down to a steady cruise around 30 mph and holds it —
actively driven, not coasting — for as long as the race runs on. It
still pulls to the side, still avoids the barrier and the rest of the
field, it just never stops.

## Everything reads in mph, feet, and pounds

The HUD speedometer, gear top speeds and nominal RPM in the designer,
braking distance, car weight, and track length/elevation/finish-line
countdown are all imperial now. The simulation underneath hasn't
changed at all — it's still entirely metric internally — only what you
actually see on screen has.

## Playing it

Download `wiikart.zip`, unzip, and open `wiikart.dol` in
[Dolphin](https://dolphin-emu.org) (File > Open). On a real Wii, copy the
`apps` folder to an SD card and launch it from the Homebrew Channel.

To edit `cars.json`, `settings.json` or `controls.json` in Dolphin you need
an SD card: Config > Wii > Insert SD Card, put the `apps` folder on it, and
launch `boot.dol` from there. The JSON CONFIG screen on the main menu tells
you what was actually read.
